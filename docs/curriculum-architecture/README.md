# Curriculum Architecture — Applied Commerce → BIS

## Purpose

This architecture makes the **curriculum the source of meaning for the reporting system**. A BIS result is only useful when the system can answer:

- What concept was taught?
- Where did that concept originate in the Applied Commerce journey?
- What did the learner actually do with it?
- What evidence was produced?
- What competency does that evidence support?
- What changed between earlier and later evidence?
- What should happen next?

## The developmental arc

**Grade 8 — Foundation:** notice self, money, work, habits, planning and future self.

**Grade 9 — Deepening:** turn those foundations into enterprise, money-flow, habit, decision, community and project capability. Grade 9's own review says the point is evidence of what changed how the learner sees or acts, not merely what was remembered. fileciteturn24file13

**Grade 10 — Widening:** move from personal behaviour into systems, value creation, finance, risk, protection and sustainable structures. The book explicitly frames activities, experiments and portfolio artefacts as evidence of growth. fileciteturn23file16

**Grade 11 — Architecting:** learn leverage, leadership, wealth architecture and legacy; deliberately connect earlier assets and liabilities to current choices. The Grade 11 book requires learners to identify evidence for the five-stage developmental arc and uses the portfolio to inspect growth across time. fileciteturn23file2 fileciteturn10file9

**Grade 12 — Integration:** combine transition, career, protection, launch and legacy into an integrated life architecture. The final capstone explicitly pulls evidence and plans from earlier grades. fileciteturn10file4

## What changes in BIS reporting

The report is no longer a metric dump.

A report should be able to classify evidence as:

- **Concept attainment** — what the learner can explain or apply now.
- **Behavioural evidence** — what the learner actually did in the investigation/field period.
- **Competency evidence** — the capability demonstrated across one or more artefacts.
- **Progression evidence** — what changed pre → post, or across repeated curriculum exposures.
- **Transfer evidence** — whether the capability survives a new context.
- **Pathway evidence** — the next useful learning/action based on demonstrated strengths and gaps.
- **Evidence confidence** — how much and what kind of evidence supports the claim.

A percentage can support a claim. It should not become the claim.

## Evidence rule

No competency claim should be generated from a single self-rating alone.

A stronger claim combines, where available:

**self-report + authored work/artefact + observed/application evidence + experiment/field evidence + transfer/review evidence.**

The system must preserve the distinction between:

- evidence that exists;
- interpretation supported by that evidence;
- a recommendation derived from the evidence.

This prevents the portfolio from becoming another dashboard.

## Ten-touchpoint instructional standard

Every module should be teachable through ten purposeful touchpoints:

1. **Discover** — establish relevance and surface the learner's current model.
2. **Explain** — introduce the first useful concept/model.
3. **Bridge** — prepare the learner for the Lab/investigation and define what evidence will matter.
4. **Interpret** — read what the Lab revealed.
5. **Model** — build the learner's own working model/equation.
6. **Apply** — use the model in a real situation.
7. **Diagnose** — examine what happened and why.
8. **Deepen/Widen** — introduce the next major conceptual layer or a new context.
9. **Review/Transfer** — compare evidence and demonstrate transfer.
10. **Integrate/Demonstrate** — produce a defensible portfolio statement, next action and pathway.

### 45-minute facilitation grammar

Default lesson allocation:

- 3 min — retrieval/relevance
- 7 min — Discover
- 10 min — Explain
- 12 min — Apply/produce
- 5 min — pair/class interpretation
- 5 min — evidence capture
- 3 min — bridge/exit

The allocation is a starting constraint, not a script. Day 3 is the explicit Learning → Lab bridge; Day 10 protects demonstration, evidence, reflection, transfer and next action.

## Competency architecture

Competencies are **evidence-backed capability families**, not personality labels:

- noticing/pattern recognition
- self-regulation
- decision reasoning
- planning/future orientation
- agency/ownership
- communication/listening
- collaboration/social intelligence
- ethical judgment
- value/customer reasoning
- systems/consequence reasoning
- experimentation/adaptation
- transfer/metacognition

These families are derived from the recurring authored tasks, outcomes, experiments, portfolios and review structures. They are not presented as an official DBE competency taxonomy.

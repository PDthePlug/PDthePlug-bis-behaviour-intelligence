# Time Lab v2 — Facilitator Delivery Guide

**Programme:** Behaviour Intelligence Series™ / Applied Commerce®
**Learning source:** TIM-LM-2.0
**Instructional standard:** Discover → Explain → Apply → Investigate → Review → Transfer
**Facilitated learning:** 10 touchpoints × 45 minutes
**Separate Lab:** Day 3 Phase A — 90 minutes

## Delivery rule

The facilitator is not delivering ten mini-lectures. Each session begins with something the learner can notice or retrieve, introduces formal language only after discovery, applies the idea to the learner's own context, creates a bounded evidence artifact, and closes with a carry-forward dependency. Learner-private responses are not read aloud as a condition of participation. Competency reporting is based on authored evidence anchors and provenance, not confidence scores or facilitator impressions.

## Day 1 — Seeing Time

**Instructional job:** create relevance

**Knowledge change:** Time use can be investigated through records; the story 'I have no time' is different from evidence of where time and attention went.

**Capability change:** Identify one personally relevant time moment and distinguish remembered time from a recordable event.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-015 — Records make patterns visible: Records reduce reliance on memory and make flows, patterns, gaps and change reviewable. Sources: AC.G8.L25, AC.G8.L38, AC.G8.L39, AC.G9.L27.
- AC-CON-021 — Leakage and unnoticed accumulation: Small repeated uses of resources can accumulate into meaningful loss; tracking makes leakage visible. Sources: AC.G8.L38, AC.G9.L27.
- AC-CON-029 — Time audit and attention allocation: Time use becomes investigable when activity is recorded; attention allocation, not memory alone, reveals where time went. Sources: AC.G8.L38, AC.G9.L38.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–5 — RETRIEVE:** What does 'not enough time' usually mean in ordinary life?
- **5–12 — DISCOVER:** Contrast a busy-feeling story with a simple recorded day.
- **12–19 — EXPLAIN:** Introduce record, allocation and time moment; do not yet reveal the full Time Pause.
- **19–23 — CHECK:** Classify examples as story, observation or unknown.
- **23–34 — APPLY:** Learner reconstructs one recent time moment.
- **34–40 — EVIDENCE:** Choose the one moment that is safe and useful to investigate.
- **40–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** State what is known, what is guessed and what to notice tomorrow.

**Facilitator move:** Use the contrast between felt busyness and a record to create curiosity; do not moralise screen use or productivity.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D1.TIME_STORY` — BASELINE: starting account of the problem.
- `TIM.D1.TIME_MOMENT` — CONTEXT: one bounded context for later investigation.
- `TIM.D1.RECORD_VS_STORY` — LEARNING_CHECK: check distinction between claim and record.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Bring one observed time moment or short record into Day 2.

**Quality checks before closing the session:**
- Can the learner explain the idea in their own words rather than repeat a definition?
- Is the evidence artifact based on an authored task rather than a facilitator-created substitute?
- Has the facilitator separated observation, interpretation, prediction and outcome?
- Has any uncertainty, missing evidence or no-opportunity condition been preserved rather than scored as failure?
- Is the next session genuinely dependent on something created or observed today?

## Day 2 — Thinking Like an Investigator

**Instructional job:** build first mental model

**Knowledge change:** Memory, observation and interpretation answer different questions; evidence becomes stronger when recorded close to the event.

**Capability change:** Convert judgements about time into reviewable observations and state what remains unknown.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-012 — Accurate thinking and consequence: Choices should be distinguished from assumptions by examining evidence, alternatives and consequences. Sources: AC.G8.L17, AC.G10.L11.
- AC-CON-015 — Records make patterns visible: Records reduce reliance on memory and make flows, patterns, gaps and change reviewable. Sources: AC.G8.L25, AC.G8.L38, AC.G8.L39, AC.G9.L27.
- AC-CON-054 — Evidence types and uncertainty: Predictions, plans, explanations, simulations, observations and outcomes answer different questions and must not be collapsed into one proof claim. Sources: AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–5 — RETRIEVE:** Return to yesterday's time moment.
- **5–11 — DISCOVER:** Compare two accounts of the same event: judgement vs record.
- **11–19 — EXPLAIN:** Observation, interpretation, evidence proximity and uncertainty.
- **19–23 — CHECK:** Classify four statements.
- **23–33 — APPLY:** Rewrite personal judgements as observable statements.
- **33–39 — EVIDENCE:** Create one clean observation and one open question.
- **39–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** Explain why the observation is stronger than the original story.

**Facilitator move:** Keep asking 'What did you actually observe?' and 'What are you adding to the record?'

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D2.OBSERVATION_CLASSIFICATION` — LEARNING_CHECK: evidence reasoning check.
- `TIM.D2.CLAIM_LIMIT` — INTERPRETATION: learner states what a record can and cannot show.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Carry a clean observation and open question into the Day 3 Lab bridge.

**Quality checks before closing the session:**

## Day 3 — Prepare the Time Investigation

**Instructional job:** prepare lab handover

**Knowledge change:** A behavioural investigation needs a bounded situation, a prediction, a safe response to test and a clear record; the method is not the same as the explanation.

**Capability change:** Prepare a personal investigation question, prediction and smallest safe test without being told what the evidence is supposed to prove.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-029 — Time audit and attention allocation: Time use becomes investigable when activity is recorded; attention allocation, not memory alone, reveals where time went. Sources: AC.G8.L38, AC.G9.L38.
- AC-CON-030 — Start friction and procrastination: Delay can arise from fear, overwhelm, perfectionism, immediate reward or genuine lack of capacity; the intervention must match the cause. Sources: AC.G9.L39.
- AC-CON-052 — Protection, failure signals and recovery: A robust plan states what could fail, what observable signal would reveal failure, and what response or support follows. Sources: AC.G10.L40, AC.G12.L50, AC.G12.L54, AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.
- AC-CON-054 — Evidence types and uncertainty: Predictions, plans, explanations, simulations, observations and outcomes answer different questions and must not be collapsed into one proof claim. Sources: AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–5 — RETRIEVE:** Observation and open question.
- **5–12 — DISCOVER:** Show why an unbounded promise such as 'manage time better' cannot be tested.
- **12–20 — EXPLAIN:** Teach investigation mechanics: situation, prediction, smallest response, evidence window. Do not give the final causal explanation.
- **20–30 — APPLY:** Draft personal test on one time moment.
- **30–38 — EVIDENCE:** Record prediction and plan before the test.
- **38–43 — CHECK:** Readiness, feasibility, privacy and no-opportunity logic.
- **43–45 — HANDOVER:** Explain the separate Lab Phase A session and return path.

**Facilitator move:** Treat the Time Pause as an investigation protocol at this point, not as proof of why the learner's pattern occurs.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D3.PREDICTION` — PREDICTION: pre-registered expectation.
- `TIM.D3.TEST_PLAN` — PLAN: experiment design.
- `TIM.D3.READINESS` — SUPPORT_SIGNAL: facilitator readiness/safety check.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Separate facilitated Lab Phase A follows this 45-minute session; it may deepen Mapping, Equation and Contract but must preserve the pre-registered prediction.

**Quality checks before closing the session:**
- Can the learner explain the idea in their own words rather than repeat a definition?
- Is the evidence artifact based on an authored task rather than a facilitator-created substitute?
- Has the facilitator separated observation, interpretation, prediction and outcome?
- Has any uncertainty, missing evidence or no-opportunity condition been preserved rather than scored as failure?
- Is the next session genuinely dependent on something created or observed today?

### Day 3 Lab boundary

After the 45-minute learning bridge, run the separate 90-minute Time Lab Phase A. Do not compress the Lab into the lesson. Do not reveal a preferred explanation before the learner records the prediction. The Lab may deepen Mapping, Equation and Contract, but the prediction remains historically intact.

## Day 4 — What the Time Pattern Revealed

**Instructional job:** interpret lab revelation

**Knowledge change:** Time patterns can involve triggers, attention pulls, start friction, environmental cues, repeated routines and small leaks; these are explanations to test, not labels.

**Capability change:** Use formal concepts to explain the learner's own mapped pattern while naming alternative explanations.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-021 — Leakage and unnoticed accumulation: Small repeated uses of resources can accumulate into meaningful loss; tracking makes leakage visible. Sources: AC.G8.L38, AC.G9.L27.
- AC-CON-022 — Habit loop: Repeated behaviour can be examined through cues, routines and rewards rather than treated as character. Sources: AC.G8.L41, AC.G8.L42.
- AC-CON-029 — Time audit and attention allocation: Time use becomes investigable when activity is recorded; attention allocation, not memory alone, reveals where time went. Sources: AC.G8.L38, AC.G9.L38.
- AC-CON-030 — Start friction and procrastination: Delay can arise from fear, overwhelm, perfectionism, immediate reward or genuine lack of capacity; the intervention must match the cause. Sources: AC.G9.L39.
- AC-CON-037 — Automation, defaults and friction: A system can make desired actions easier and repeated decisions less dependent on willpower by changing defaults or friction. Sources: AC.G10.L39, AC.G11.L09.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–6 — RETRIEVE:** What did the Lab make visible that the learner did not see on Day 1?
- **6–13 — DISCOVER:** Compare two mapped time moments with different mechanisms.
- **13–22 — EXPLAIN:** Name trigger, pull, leak, start friction, smallest first step and environmental protection.
- **22–26 — CHECK:** Choose the best explanation for short cases and explain why.
- **26–36 — APPLY:** Annotate the learner's own map with formal concepts.
- **36–41 — EVIDENCE:** Write a working explanation plus one competing explanation.
- **41–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** Identify what real-world evidence would separate the explanations.

**Facilitator move:** Make explanation feel like recognition of what the learner already encountered, not a lecture detached from the Lab.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D4.WORKING_EXPLANATION` — INTERPRETATION: first formal explanation after Lab discovery.
- `TIM.D4.CONCEPT_DISCRIMINATION` — LEARNING_CHECK: distinguish trigger, leak, friction and condition.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Watch the next eligible moments for evidence that supports or weakens the working explanation.

**Quality checks before closing the session:**

## Day 5 — Evidence Studio

**Instructional job:** build personal model

**Knowledge change:** Eligible opportunity, missed response, partial response and no opportunity are different records; honest denominators matter more than a flattering rate.

**Capability change:** Record early experiment evidence accurately and diagnose evidence-quality problems without changing history.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-015 — Records make patterns visible: Records reduce reliance on memory and make flows, patterns, gaps and change reviewable. Sources: AC.G8.L25, AC.G8.L38, AC.G8.L39, AC.G9.L27.
- AC-CON-026 — Consistency, setbacks and recovery: Progress does not require perfection; recovery after disruption is part of a sustainable behaviour system. Sources: AC.G8.L47, AC.G8.L48, AC.G8.L49.
- AC-CON-052 — Protection, failure signals and recovery: A robust plan states what could fail, what observable signal would reveal failure, and what response or support follows. Sources: AC.G10.L40, AC.G12.L50, AC.G12.L54, AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.
- AC-CON-054 — Evidence types and uncertainty: Predictions, plans, explanations, simulations, observations and outcomes answer different questions and must not be collapsed into one proof claim. Sources: AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–5 — RETRIEVE:** Return to prediction and first observations.
- **5–11 — DISCOVER:** Compare honest no-opportunity data with a misleading perfect record.
- **11–19 — EXPLAIN:** Opportunity denominator, missingness, partial response and evidence integrity.
- **19–23 — CHECK:** Classify sample tracker rows.
- **23–33 — APPLY:** Review own rows without editing what happened.
- **33–39 — EVIDENCE:** Resolve recording ambiguity and mark unknowns.
- **39–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** State what early evidence can and cannot show.

**Facilitator move:** Reward accurate recording, including failure and no opportunity; never reward 'good-looking' data.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D5.EARLY_OBSERVATIONS` — OBSERVATION: real-world test records.
- `TIM.D5.EVIDENCE_QUALITY_REVIEW` — INTERPRETATION: record-quality review.

## Day 6 — Apply Under Real Conditions

**Instructional job:** apply in real life

**Knowledge change:** A planned response must survive ordinary conditions; environment, capacity and first-step size can change follow-through.

**Capability change:** Use the planned response in real eligible moments and record what happened without retrospective score-making.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-023 — Repetition and automaticity: Repeated responses can become easier or more automatic; the conditions around repetition matter. Sources: AC.G8.L42, AC.G8.L44, AC.G9.L36.
- AC-CON-026 — Consistency, setbacks and recovery: Progress does not require perfection; recovery after disruption is part of a sustainable behaviour system. Sources: AC.G8.L47, AC.G8.L48, AC.G8.L49.
- AC-CON-030 — Start friction and procrastination: Delay can arise from fear, overwhelm, perfectionism, immediate reward or genuine lack of capacity; the intervention must match the cause. Sources: AC.G9.L39.
- AC-CON-037 — Automation, defaults and friction: A system can make desired actions easier and repeated decisions less dependent on willpower by changing defaults or friction. Sources: AC.G10.L39, AC.G11.L09.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–5 — RETRIEVE:** What is the plan, exactly?
- **5–10 — DISCOVER:** One case where the same plan works in one condition and fails in another.
- **10–17 — EXPLAIN:** Condition dependence, smallest first step and environmental support.
- **17–21 — CHECK:** Which condition should change first?
- **21–31 — APPLY:** Rehearse next eligible moment.
- **31–38 — EVIDENCE:** Review authentic observations already captured.
- **38–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** Name one condition to watch, not yet a permanent change to the contract.

**Facilitator move:** Keep the focus on applying the pre-registered method; do not rescue the learner by rewriting the experiment every day.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D6.REAL_WORLD_APPLICATION` — OBSERVATION: authentic-context application.
- `TIM.D6.RESPONSE_RESULT` — OUTCOME: what happened after planned response.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Collect the next observations with condition notes where relevant.

## Day 7 — Experiment Clinic

**Instructional job:** diagnose and adapt

**Knowledge change:** A barrier is information about conditions, resources or design; a justified adjustment differs from moving the goalposts.

**Capability change:** Compare successful and unsuccessful opportunities, diagnose one barrier and make one traceable adjustment.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-011 — Failure as information: A failed attempt can provide evidence about assumptions, design or conditions when the learner examines what happened. Sources: AC.G8.L15, AC.G8.L50, AC.G9.L42.
- AC-CON-026 — Consistency, setbacks and recovery: Progress does not require perfection; recovery after disruption is part of a sustainable behaviour system. Sources: AC.G8.L47, AC.G8.L48, AC.G8.L49.
- AC-CON-032 — Resilience as internal and external resources: Recovery depends on both internal capacities and external resources; barriers are not automatically evidence of weak character. Sources: AC.G9.L40, AC.G12.L54, AC.G12.L58.
- AC-CON-052 — Protection, failure signals and recovery: A robust plan states what could fail, what observable signal would reveal failure, and what response or support follows. Sources: AC.G10.L40, AC.G12.L50, AC.G12.L54, AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–6 — RETRIEVE:** Select one worked and one difficult opportunity.
- **6–12 — DISCOVER:** Side-by-side comparison reveals condition differences.
- **12–19 — EXPLAIN:** Barrier vs failure; capacity vs avoidance; legitimate adjustment vs result-chasing.
- **19–23 — CHECK:** Judge whether sample adjustments preserve the test.
- **23–34 — APPLY:** Diagnose own evidence.
- **34–40 — EVIDENCE:** Record one justified adjustment with reason.
- **40–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** Predict what the adjustment should change.

**Facilitator move:** Ask for evidence before accepting a diagnosis; preserve the original plan and adjustment history.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D7.BARRIER_DIAGNOSIS` — INTERPRETATION: comparison of evidence.
- `TIM.D7.JUSTIFIED_ADJUSTMENT` — PLAN: versioned experiment adjustment.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Run the adjusted response through the remaining window and keep the original prediction available for final review.

## Day 8 — Time Leverage and Protection

**Instructional job:** add final major layer

**Knowledge change:** Managing one time moment is different from redesigning a time system; priority, batching, elimination, delegation, automation and protection can change the system but also create costs and responsibilities.

**Capability change:** Identify one leverage point in a time system, predict its ripple effect and choose an age/context-appropriate intervention.

**Applied Commerce ancestry:**
- AC-CON-028 — Planning and prioritisation: Planning converts priorities into ordered actions and protects what matters from lower-value demands. Sources: AC.G8.L56, AC.G9.L38, AC.G12.L15.
- AC-CON-036 — Systems thinking: Outcomes emerge from connected parts, relationships and feedback; changing one part can create wider effects. Sources: AC.G10.L37, AC.G10.L38.
- AC-CON-038 — Leverage points: Some points in a system produce disproportionate effects; leverage should be identified, tested and monitored for unintended consequences. Sources: AC.G10.L37, AC.G10.L40, AC.G11.L14.
- AC-CON-047 — Time leverage: Time can be multiplied through prioritisation, batching, systematisation, automation, delegation and elimination, while accountability remains. Sources: AC.G9.L38, AC.G11.L09.
- AC-CON-052 — Protection, failure signals and recovery: A robust plan states what could fail, what observable signal would reveal failure, and what response or support follows. Sources: AC.G10.L40, AC.G12.L50, AC.G12.L54, AC.G12.L59, AC.G12.L60.

**45-minute delivery:**
- **0–5 — RETRIEVE:** What has the experiment changed at the moment level?
- **5–12 — DISCOVER:** Contrast trying harder with redesigning a weekly system.
- **12–21 — EXPLAIN:** Priority, batching, elimination, systematisation, automation, delegation and accountability.
- **21–25 — CHECK:** Choose leverage methods appropriate to cases.
- **25–35 — APPLY:** Map one weekly system and find a leverage point.
- **35–40 — EVIDENCE:** Predict a ripple and name a risk or cost.
- **40–45 — REVIEW CARRY:** Choose whether this is a future transfer test, not a mid-experiment rewrite.

**Facilitator move:** Broaden capability without implying every learner can delegate, automate or remove structural obligations.

**Evidence created:**
- `TIM.D8.LEVERAGE_REASONING` — LEARNING_CHECK: systems/time leverage understanding.
- `TIM.D8.LEVERAGE_PLAN` — PLAN: bounded leverage-point application.

**Carry-forward dependency:** Bring the completed seven-day record and leverage idea to evidence review.

**Quality checks before closing the session:**

## Reporting handoff

At programme close, the learner-facing report should describe starting point, concepts learned, evidence of application, what the experiment showed, revision, transfer, current limit and next action. Competency claims stop at the highest level supported by authored evidence. Sponsor reporting receives privacy-safe aggregates only and must not expose private time patterns or individual reflections.
